Question 1.Apply · Measurement scales

A hotel records guest satisfaction on a 5-point scale (1 = very dissatisfied … 5 = very satisfied). An analyst reports a mean of 4.0 for Hotel A and 2.0 for Hotel B and concludes that Hotel A guests are “twice as satisfied”. What is wrong with this conclusion?

  • A Rating scores have no true zero and equal spacing is assumed, so a “twice as” claim is unsupported ✓ correct
  • B A 5-point scale is too short, and a 10-point scale would make a ratio claim like this one perfectly valid
  • C The comparison fails only because the two hotels almost certainly had different numbers of survey respondents
  • D Means cannot be calculated for any rating-scale data, so neither of the two hotel figures can be used at all

Correct answer A: Rating scores have no true zero and equal spacing is assumed, so a “twice as” claim is unsupported.

Why this is right.

Ratio statements (“twice as”) need a true zero and equal intervals. A rating of 1 does not mean zero satisfaction, and the gap between “neutral” and “satisfied” is not guaranteed to equal the gap between “dissatisfied” and “neutral”. You can say Hotel A scored 2 points higher on average, not that it is twice as satisfied.

The trap.

Many students decide that means are forbidden for Likert data. In practice, means of multi-item rating scales are widely used as approximately interval; what you cannot do is make ratio claims.

Question 2.Apply · Nominal data

A survey codes country of residence as 1 = Singapore, 2 = Malaysia, 3 = Indonesia, 4 = Other. A student computes a mean country code of 2.1 for the class. Which summary would actually be meaningful for this variable?

  • A The frequency or percentage in each category, together with the mode (the most common category) ✓ correct
  • B The median code, because the median is not affected by extreme values and is therefore safe for any variable
  • C The mean code rounded to the nearest whole number, so that it points to one of the four countries
  • D The standard deviation of the codes, which would show how diverse the class is across the countries

Correct answer A: The frequency or percentage in each category, together with the mode (the most common category).

Why this is right.

Codes for nominal categories are labels, not quantities, so arithmetic on them is meaningless: a mean of 2.1 does not describe any country. Counts, percentages and the mode respect the fact that the categories have no order and no distance between them.

The trap.

The median looks safe because it only needs an order, but nominal categories have no order, so even the median is undefined.

Question 3.Concept · Ordinal vs interval

An airline loyalty programme has four tiers: Silver, Gold, Platinum and Diamond. Why is tier an ordinal variable rather than an interval variable?

  • A Silver is the lowest tier, so the scale has a true zero point and is therefore ratio rather than interval
  • B A customer can belong to only one tier at a time, and that exclusivity is what makes a variable ordinal
  • C The tiers are words rather than numbers, so no arithmetic or ranking of any kind is possible with them
  • D The tiers have a rank order, but the steps between neighbouring tiers are not known to be equal ✓ correct

Correct answer D: The tiers have a rank order, but the steps between neighbouring tiers are not known to be equal.

Why this is right.

Ordinal scales preserve order but not distance. Moving from Silver to Gold may need 10,000 points while Platinum to Diamond needs 50,000. Because the intervals are unequal and unknown, means and differences between tiers are not interpretable; medians, modes and rank-based tests are.

The trap.

Being labelled with words does not make a variable nominal. What matters is whether the labels carry an order (ordinal) and, beyond that, equal spacing (interval).

Question 4.Apply · Interval vs ratio

A factory oven reads 200°C at 9 am and 400°C at 10 am. A supervisor says the oven is now “twice as hot”. Which statement is correct?

  • A The claim is valid, because 400 is exactly twice 200 on every temperature scale that physicists use
  • B The claim is invalid because temperature is a nominal variable and cannot be compared numerically
  • C The claim is valid only if the oven was read with a thermometer that has high enough precision
  • D Celsius has an arbitrary zero, so “twice as hot” is invalid; in Kelvin the ratio is only about 1.4 ✓ correct

Correct answer D: Celsius has an arbitrary zero, so “twice as hot” is invalid; in Kelvin the ratio is only about 1.4.

Why this is right.

Ratios only make sense when zero means “none of the quantity”. 0°C is simply the freezing point of water, so Celsius has equal intervals but an arbitrary zero. Absolute zero (0 K) is the true zero. Converting: 200°C = 473.15 K and 400°C = 673.15 K, a ratio of 673.15 ÷ 473.15 ≈ 1.42.

The trap.

Precision is not the issue. Even a perfect thermometer cannot make ratios valid on a scale whose zero is arbitrary.

Question 5.Calculate · Mean vs median

Monthly revenue (RM thousand) of seven SMEs in a district is 18, 22, 25, 27, 30, 31 and 2,400 (a subsidiary of a listed group). A policy analyst wants one number for the “typical” SME. Which should she report?

  • A The mean, about RM365 thousand, because it is the only average that uses every one of the observations
  • B The median, RM27 thousand, because the mean (about RM365 thousand) is pulled up by one extreme value ✓ correct
  • C The range, RM2,382 thousand, because it shows the full picture of how different the SMEs are
  • D The mode, because it is the most common value and therefore the best description of a typical firm

Correct answer B: The median, RM27 thousand, because the mean (about RM365 thousand) is pulled up by one extreme value.

Why this is right.

The mean uses every value, so a single RM2.4 million observation pulls it to about RM365 thousand, which is larger than six of the seven firms by an order of magnitude. The median is the middle ranked value, RM27 thousand, and describes the typical firm. That is why income, revenue and house-price data are usually summarised by medians.

The trap.

“Uses every observation” sounds like a virtue, but it is exactly what makes the mean fragile with skewed data. The mode does not exist here because no value repeats.

Question 6.Interpret · Skew from mean and median

Customer waiting times at a bank branch have a mean of 14 minutes and a median of 9 minutes. What does this most likely indicate about the distribution?

  • A It is positively skewed: a minority of very long waits pull the mean above the median ✓ correct
  • B It is symmetric, because the mean and median are both reasonable measures of the centre
  • C It is negatively skewed, because the mean is the larger of the two centre values
  • D It is bimodal, because the mean and the median are so far apart from one another

Correct answer A: It is positively skewed: a minority of very long waits pull the mean above the median.

Why this is right.

When the mean exceeds the median, the right tail is longer: a few very large values stretch the mean upwards while the median stays at the middle observation. Queues, incomes and claim sizes typically look like this. The typical customer waits about 9 minutes; the average of 14 is inflated by the unlucky few.

The trap.

Do not read “positive” as “good”. Positive skew means the tail points towards the positive (right) end of the axis, not that the outcome is favourable.

Question 7.Apply · Summaries for ordinal data

A café records drink-size orders over a week: Small 120, Medium 340, Large 210, Extra-large 30. Which summary is appropriate for this ordinal variable?

  • A The mode and the median are both Medium; a mean size would not be meaningful for ordered categories ✓ correct
  • B The median is Large, because Large is the middle one of the four size categories on the menu
  • C Only the mode can be used, because an ordinal variable has no meaningful middle value to report
  • D The mean size is about 2.2, which sits between Medium and Large and describes the average order

Correct answer A: The mode and the median are both Medium; a mean size would not be meaningful for ordered categories.

Why this is right.

Ordinal data support the mode and the median, because both depend only on order and counts. With 700 orders the middle observation is the 350th; the cumulative count is 120 after Small and 460 after Medium, so the median is Medium, as is the mode (340 orders). A mean of size codes (about 2.2) assumes equal gaps between sizes, which ordinal data cannot guarantee.

The trap.

The median is not “the middle category”; it is the category containing the middle observation, so it depends on the frequencies.

Question 8.Apply · Composite scores

A researcher averages four 5-point Likert items into one “service quality” score and then reports its mean and standard deviation. What assumption is being made?

  • A That no assumption is needed, because averaging always converts ordinal responses into interval data
  • B That the composite is a nominal variable, so only the frequency of each average value is meaningful
  • C That the composite is approximately interval, more defensible when its items form a reliable scale ✓ correct
  • D That the composite is a ratio variable, because averaging several items creates a true zero point

Correct answer C: That the composite is approximately interval, more defensible when its items form a reliable scale.

Why this is right.

Single Likert items are strictly ordinal. Averaging several items from a reliable scale gives a score with many possible values that behaves approximately like an interval variable, which is the convention in management research. It remains an assumption, so researchers acknowledge it in the methodology chapter.

The trap.

Averaging does not create a true zero or exact equal spacing. It makes the interval assumption more reasonable, not proven.

Question 9.Diagnose · Reliability vs validity

A “customer loyalty” questionnaire gives almost identical scores when the same customers complete it two weeks apart, but all of its items ask how convenient the store location is. Which statement is correct?

  • A The measure is neither reliable nor valid, because it only asks about a single topic of the store
  • B The measure is both reliable and valid, because stable scores prove that it measures loyalty well
  • C The measure is reliable but not valid, because it consistently measures the wrong construct ✓ correct
  • D The measure is valid but not reliable, because convenient locations are a strong driver of loyalty

Correct answer C: The measure is reliable but not valid, because it consistently measures the wrong construct.

Why this is right.

Reliability is consistency; here the scores are stable across time (test–retest reliability). Validity is whether the instrument measures what it claims to measure. Location convenience is not loyalty, so the scores are consistent but answer a different question. Reliability is necessary for validity, but never sufficient.

The trap.

A stable score feels like proof of a good instrument. A bathroom scale that is always 5 kg off is perfectly reliable and invalid.

Question 10.Apply · Statistic vs parameter

An analyst computes the mean daily return of the S&P 500 over the latest 99 trading days and uses it to describe the index’s long-run average daily return. In statistical terms, the 99-day mean is:

  • A A sample statistic that estimates the unknown population parameter ✓ correct
  • B A sampling error, because it will differ from the true long-run average return
  • C A population parameter, because it describes the actual prices of the index itself
  • D A variable, because it takes a different value on every one of the trading days

Correct answer A: A sample statistic that estimates the unknown population parameter.

Why this is right.

A parameter describes the entire population (every daily return the index will ever produce); a statistic describes the sample we happened to observe. The latest 99 days are one sample from that process, so their mean is an estimate that would differ if we had chosen another window. The gap between estimate and truth is the sampling error.

The trap.

Do not confuse the estimate with its error. The statistic is the number you computed; the sampling error is how far it sits from the unknown truth.

Question 11.Apply · Variable types

A study records (1) number of employees, (2) industry sector, (3) annual revenue in RM and (4) digital-maturity stage (1 = none … 4 = fully transformed). Which classification is correct?

  • A Ratio, ordinal, interval, interval
  • B Interval, nominal, ratio, nominal
  • C Ratio, nominal, ratio, ordinal ✓ correct
  • D Ratio, nominal, interval, ordinal

Correct answer C: Ratio, nominal, ratio, ordinal.

Why this is right.

Employees and revenue have a true zero (no employees, no revenue), so ratio statements are valid. Industry sector has no order: nominal. Maturity stage is ordered, but the effort between stages is unequal: ordinal.

The trap.

Revenue is sometimes mislabelled “interval” because it is measured in currency units. But a firm with RM0 revenue truly has none, and RM2 million really is twice RM1 million.

Question 12.Diagnose · Operationalisation

To measure “employee engagement”, a researcher uses a single indicator: the number of days of absence last year. Which criticism is most serious?

  • A The wrong scale, because absence days must always be measured on an ordinal scale instead
  • B Low reliability, because the number of absence days is always recorded inaccurately by firms
  • C Non-response bias, because employees who are absent cannot be sampled for the study at all
  • D Poor content validity: engagement is multi-dimensional and absence captures only a sliver of it ✓ correct

Correct answer D: Poor content validity: engagement is multi-dimensional and absence captures only a sliver of it.

Why this is right.

Operationalisation is the bridge between a concept and its measure. Engagement includes vigour, dedication and absorption; days absent may reflect illness, caring duties or policy. A single narrow proxy under-represents the construct (content validity) and mixes in unrelated causes. Use a validated multi-item scale.

The trap.

The numbers may be perfectly accurate and ratio-scaled. The weakness is not precision but whether the number means engagement.

Question 13.Interpret · Multimodal data

A histogram of monthly salaries in a firm shows two clear peaks, around RM3,000 and RM9,000, with a mean of RM6,000. What is the most informative way to report this?

  • A Report the median alone, because the median ignores both the lower and the upper salary peaks
  • B Report both modes and investigate the two groups, since a mean of RM6,000 describes almost nobody ✓ correct
  • C Remove the RM9,000 group as outliers so that the remaining data become a single-peaked shape
  • D Report the mean of RM6,000 alone, because it is a single figure that summarises all employees

Correct answer B: Report both modes and investigate the two groups, since a mean of RM6,000 describes almost nobody.

Why this is right.

Two peaks (bimodality) usually mean two distinct sub-populations, for example operators and executives or two job grades. A single central value falls in the valley between them, where few employees sit. Describe each group separately, or add a grouping variable, before computing summary statistics.

The trap.

Deleting data to make a picture tidy is not analysis. Bimodality is a finding, not a defect.

Question 14.Interpret · Percentile rank

A student scores at the 85th percentile in a national research-methods test. What does this mean?

  • A The student answered 85% of the questions correctly in the test
  • B About 85% of test takers scored at or below this student’s score ✓ correct
  • C The student scored 85 marks out of a possible 100 marks overall
  • D About 85% of the test takers scored above this particular student

Correct answer B: About 85% of test takers scored at or below this student’s score.

Why this is right.

A percentile is a rank-based position, not a raw score: the 85th percentile means roughly 85% of the reference group scored the same or lower, leaving about 15% above. A student at the 85th percentile may have answered anywhere from 40% to 95% of the questions correctly, depending on how hard the test was.

The trap.

Percent correct and percentile rank are different things. A percentile tells you where you stand relative to others, not how much you got right.

Question 15.Calculate · Updating a mean

The mean mark of 20 students is 70. A late submission is marked 91 and added to the class. What is the new mean?

  • A 70.5
  • B 75
  • C 71 ✓ correct
  • D 80.5

Correct answer C: 71.

Why this is right.

New mean = (sum of old marks + new mark) ÷ new number of students = (20 × 70 + 91) ÷ 21 = 1,491 ÷ 21 = 71. The new mark is 21 points above the old mean, but spread across 21 students it moves the mean by 21 ÷ 21 = 1 point.

The trap.

Averaging the old mean and the new mark, (70 + 91) ÷ 2 = 80.5, ignores that the old mean represents 20 students while the new mark represents just one.

Question 16.Apply · Matching the average to the scale

A survey records three variables: preferred payment app (GrabPay, PayNow, card), loyalty tier (Silver to Diamond) and an exam score out of 100 that is roughly symmetric. Which choice gives the most appropriate measure of the centre for each, in that order?

  • A Mode, mean, median
  • B Median, mode, mean
  • C Mean, median, mode
  • D Mode, median, mean ✓ correct

Correct answer D: Mode, median, mean.

Why this is right.

Choose the centre that respects the scale. Payment app is nominal, so only the mode (the most frequent category) is meaningful. Loyalty tier is ordinal, so the median respects the order without assuming equal gaps. A roughly symmetric exam score is interval or ratio with no extreme values, so the mean uses all the information. If the scores were heavily skewed, the median would be the safer choice.

The trap.

The mean is not the “best” average in general. It is best only when the scale is interval or ratio and the data are not badly skewed.

Question 17.Interpret · Which way the tail pulls the mean

In an exam, most students score between 70 and 90, but a handful score below 30. The histogram has a long tail on the left. Which statement about the mean and median is most likely correct?

  • A The mean and the median are equal, because most of the marks are bunched closely together
  • B The mean is lower than the median, because the few very low marks pull the mean down ✓ correct
  • C The mean is higher than the median, because most students scored well above the middle
  • D The mean cannot be compared with the median, because the distribution has a long tail

Correct answer B: The mean is lower than the median, because the few very low marks pull the mean down.

Why this is right.

A long tail on the left is negative (left) skew. Extreme low values drag the mean towards the tail while the median stays near the bulk of the data, so the mean is below the median. It is the mirror image of the waiting-time example, where a right tail pushes the mean above the median.

The trap.

The direction of the tail, not the position of the bulk of the data, tells you which way the mean is pulled.

Quiz 2 — Distribution & Correlation · Intermediate · 18 questions

Question 1.Calculate · Normal distribution

Two production lines both average a quality score of 85. Line A has SD = 3 and Line B has SD = 12. Assuming roughly normal scores, between which two values do about 95% of Line A’s scores fall?

  • A 76 to 94
  • B 79 to 91 ✓ correct
  • C 73 to 97
  • D 82 to 88

Correct answer B: 79 to 91.

Why this is right.

The empirical rule: about 68% of values lie within 1 SD of the mean, 95% within 2 SDs and 99.7% within 3 SDs (SD = standard deviation). For Line A (mean 85, SD 3), 85 ± 2 × 3 gives 79 to 91. A small SD makes Line A highly predictable; Line B’s 95% band (61 to 109) is four times as wide.

The trap.

Each wrong option answers a different question: 82 to 88 is Line A’s 68% band, 73 to 97 is Line B’s 68% band, and 76 to 94 is Line A’s 99.7% band.

Question 2.Calculate · Outlier fences

Delivery times have Q1 = 60 minutes and Q3 = 80 minutes. Using Tukey’s 1.5 × IQR rule, is a delivery of 115 minutes an outlier?

  • A No: the upper fence is 80 + 1.5 × 80 = 200, so any delivery below 200 minutes is acceptable
  • B No: outliers only begin 3 × IQR beyond Q3, which is any delivery longer than 140 minutes
  • C Yes: the upper fence is 80 + 1.5 × 20 = 110, and 115 lies beyond it ✓ correct
  • D Yes: every delivery longer than 100 minutes counts as an outlier, whatever the quartiles are

Correct answer C: Yes: the upper fence is 80 + 1.5 × 20 = 110, and 115 lies beyond it.

Why this is right.

IQR (interquartile range) = Q3 − Q1 = 20, where Q1 and Q3 are the 25th and 75th percentiles. Tukey’s fences sit 1.5 IQRs beyond the quartiles: lower = 60 − 30 = 30, upper = 80 + 30 = 110. Values outside the fences are flagged as outliers (the dots on a boxplot). Since 115 > 110, it is flagged.

The trap.

The rule uses the IQR, not Q3 itself. And “flagged” does not mean “wrong”: check whether it is an error, a special case or genuine data.

Question 3.Interpret · Skewness

SPSS reports a skewness of +1.8 for response times in a customer-service study. Which statement is correct?

  • A The distribution is flatter than a normal curve, with fewer extreme values in either tail
  • B The distribution is symmetric, because skewness is not zero but is still below a value of 2
  • C The distribution has a long left tail, and the mean is likely to be below the median
  • D The distribution has a long right tail, and the mean is likely to exceed the median ✓ correct

Correct answer D: The distribution has a long right tail, and the mean is likely to exceed the median.

Why this is right.

Skewness measures asymmetry. A positive value means the bulk of the data sits on the left with a long tail stretching to the right, so extreme high values pull the mean above the median. Zero indicates symmetry. Many applied researchers treat values between −2 and +2 as acceptable for approximate normality, but +1.8 is clearly visible skew and worth reporting.

The trap.

“Flatter than normal” describes kurtosis, not skewness. Skewness is about direction of the tail; kurtosis is about tail weight.

Question 4.Interpret · Kurtosis in markets

Daily gold returns show an excess kurtosis of +6. What does this imply for risk assessment?

  • A Low volatility: the returns cluster tightly so that big moves in either direction are unlikely
  • B Positive skew: large daily gains are more likely than large daily losses of similar size
  • C Thin tails: extreme daily moves are rarer than a normal distribution would suggest
  • D Fat tails: extreme daily moves occur much more often than a normal distribution would predict ✓ correct

Correct answer D: Fat tails: extreme daily moves occur much more often than a normal distribution would predict.

Why this is right.

Excess kurtosis compares tail weight with a normal distribution (excess kurtosis = kurtosis − 3, so a normal distribution scores 0). Positive values (leptokurtic) mean more probability in the tails and a sharper centre. For gold, SPX or NDX this means 4- and 5-standard-deviation days happen far more often than a normal model predicts, so risk models and option prices that assume normal returns understate tail risk.

The trap.

Kurtosis is about tail weight in both directions and says nothing about which direction. Direction is skewness.

Question 5.Diagnose · Normality testing

With n = 500 survey responses, a Shapiro–Wilk test gives p < .001, yet skewness = 0.3, excess kurtosis = 0.4 and the histogram is clearly bell-shaped. What is the best decision?

  • A Collect a much smaller sample so that the normality test has less power to find small departures
  • B Treat it as approximately normal; big samples flag trivial departures, so rely on skew, kurtosis and plots ✓ correct
  • C Reject normality and switch to non-parametric tests, because p < .001 proves the data are not normal
  • D Delete the 20 most extreme responses repeatedly until the normality test is no longer significant

Correct answer B: Treat it as approximately normal; big samples flag trivial departures, so rely on skew, kurtosis and plots.

Why this is right.

Normality tests become very sensitive as n grows: even tiny, harmless departures give p < .001. What matters is the size and shape of the departure. Skewness of 0.3 and excess kurtosis of 0.4 are very small, the histogram looks normal, and with n = 500 the sampling distribution of the mean is normal by the central limit theorem (CLT).

The trap.

A significant normality test is not the same as a practically important departure. Trimming data until a test passes is data manipulation.

Question 6.Calculate · Normal probability

Monthly online spending by customers is approximately normal with mean RM200 and SD RM40. About what proportion of customers spend more than RM280?

  • A About 2.5% ✓ correct
  • B About 0.15%
  • C About 5%
  • D About 16%

Correct answer A: About 2.5%.

Why this is right.

RM280 is (280 − 200) ÷ 40 = 2 SDs above the mean (200 is the mean and 40 the SD). About 95% of a normal distribution lies within ±2 SDs, leaving about 5% in the two tails combined, so roughly 2.5% (more exactly 2.3%) lies above +2 SD.

The trap.

5% is the proportion beyond 2 SDs on either side; the question asks only about the upper tail, which is half of that. 16% is the share above +1 SD and 0.15% the share above +3 SD.

Question 7.Interpret · Central limit theorem

Revenue per SME is strongly right-skewed. You can survey either 12 SMEs or 150 SMEs. Which statement about the sampling distribution of the mean is most accurate?

  • A With 150 SMEs it will be close to normal; with 12 it may remain visibly skewed ✓ correct
  • B It will be normal for both sample sizes, because the central limit theorem works for any n
  • C It will be skewed for both sample sizes, because the population itself is strongly skewed
  • D It will be normal for 12 but skewed for 150, because small samples average out the skew

Correct answer A: With 150 SMEs it will be close to normal; with 12 it may remain visibly skewed.

Why this is right.

The central limit theorem says that as n grows, the distribution of sample means approaches a normal distribution whatever the population shape. How fast depends on the skew: n ≈ 30 is a common rule of thumb, but heavily skewed variables such as revenue may need 100 or more. With n = 12 the sampling distribution of the mean can still be noticeably skewed.

The trap.

The CLT is a “for large enough n” result. It does not give automatic protection for tiny samples.

Question 8.Diagnose · CLT misconception

A student writes: “Because n = 200, the central limit theorem makes my income data normally distributed.” What is the error?

  • A The CLT only applies when n is above 1,000, so a sample of 200 is far too small
  • B There is no error: large samples turn any kind of data into normally distributed data
  • C The CLT applies only to percentages and proportions, and never to continuous variables such as incomes
  • D The CLT concerns the distribution of sample means, not the raw data, which keep their skewed shape ✓ correct

Correct answer D: The CLT concerns the distribution of sample means, not the raw data, which keep their skewed shape.

Why this is right.

A histogram of the 200 incomes will still be skewed. What becomes approximately normal is the distribution of the mean across repeated samples of size 200. That is why t-tests and regression on means remain valid, but the statement “the data are normal” remains false.

The trap.

“The data are normal” and “the sampling distribution of the mean is normal” are different claims. Examiners notice.

Question 9.Calculate · r and r²

The correlation between waiting time and satisfaction in a hotel study is r = −0.72. What proportion of the variance in satisfaction is shared with waiting time?

  • A About 72%
  • B About 85%
  • C About 52% ✓ correct
  • D About 28%

Correct answer C: About 52%.

Why this is right.

The coefficient of determination r² is the square of the correlation coefficient r (r = Pearson correlation). (−0.72)² = 0.518, so about 52% of the variation in satisfaction is linearly associated with waiting time and about 48% is not. The negative sign shows direction only; it disappears when squared.

The trap.

r = 0.72 does not mean “72% explained”. Because r² is smaller than |r|, correlations look more impressive than they are. (72% is r itself, 28% is 1 − r and 85% is the square root of r.)

Question 10.Diagnose · Confounding

Across 120 students, the number of AI study tools used correlates r = +0.45 with exam score. A student concludes that using more AI tools raises exam scores. Which alternative explanation is most plausible?

  • A Correlations measured on students always show reversed signs when the outcome is an exam
  • B A confounder such as motivation or prior ability could drive both heavy tool use and higher scores ✓ correct
  • C A count variable such as number of tools cannot be correlated with a score, so r is meaningless
  • D The correlation is too weak to be taken seriously, because r is below the value of 0.50

Correct answer B: A confounder such as motivation or prior ability could drive both heavy tool use and higher scores.

Why this is right.

Correlation shows that two variables move together, not why. Motivated or higher-ability students may adopt more tools and also score higher, so tool use and scores are both outcomes of a third variable. Reverse causation is also possible (strong students experiment more). Establishing cause needs a design that controls for these: randomisation, longitudinal data or statistical control.

The trap.

An r of about 0.45 is a moderate, genuinely useful correlation. The weakness is not its size but the causal reading.

Question 11.Diagnose · Outliers and correlation

For 30 hotels, advertising spend and revenue show virtually no relationship (r ≈ 0.05). One luxury resort with extreme spend and extreme revenue is added and r jumps to 0.60. What is the best response?

  • A Conclude that advertising works for luxury hotels, since the correlation is now a strong one overall
  • B Treat it as fragile: report r with and without the resort and consider Spearman’s rank correlation ✓ correct
  • C Report r = 0.60 as the headline result, because adding more data always makes a correlation more accurate
  • D Delete the resort permanently, because every outlier is a data-entry error and should be removed from the data

Correct answer B: Treat it as fragile: report r with and without the resort and consider Spearman’s rank correlation.

Why this is right.

Pearson’s r uses raw distances from the means, so one high-leverage point far from the rest can dominate it. A correlation that depends on a single case is not a pattern across hotels. Show the scatterplot, report results with and without the case, and check a rank-based or robust alternative.

The trap.

Removing a point just because it hurts (or helps) the result is unjustified; the point may be valid and informative. Report both versions.

Question 12.Interpret · Non-linear relationships

A scatterplot of work stress against performance forms a clear inverted U (best performance at moderate stress), yet Pearson’s r is about 0. What should you conclude?

  • A Spearman’s rho would be high, because rank correlations detect every kind of pattern in data
  • B There is no relationship between stress and performance, because r is approximately zero
  • C The data must contain errors, because a clear visible pattern always produces a high r value
  • D A strong but non-linear relationship; Pearson’s r only captures straight-line association ✓ correct

Correct answer D: A strong but non-linear relationship; Pearson’s r only captures straight-line association.

Why this is right.

Pearson’s r measures the strength of a straight-line relationship. In an inverted U, the positive slope on the left cancels the negative slope on the right, so r ≈ 0 although knowing the stress level tells you a lot about performance. Always plot the data before trusting a coefficient; test a quadratic term if theory suggests a curve.

The trap.

Spearman’s rho captures monotonic (always rising or always falling) relationships, so it too would be near zero for a U or inverted U.

Question 13.Apply · Pearson or Spearman

A researcher has two variables: customer loyalty tier (Silver to Diamond, ranked 1–4) and satisfaction rank among 80 customers. Which correlation is most appropriate?

  • A Spearman’s rho, because both variables are ordinal ✓ correct
  • B Cramér’s V, because the loyalty tiers are categories and should be treated as nominal
  • C Pearson’s r, because both variables are stored as numeric codes in the data file
  • D A point-biserial correlation, because one of the variables has only a few distinct values

Correct answer A: Spearman’s rho, because both variables are ordinal.

Why this is right.

Spearman’s rho is Pearson’s correlation applied to ranks, so it needs only ordinal information and is resistant to outliers; it detects monotonic relationships. Pearson’s r assumes interval-level measurement and a linear relationship.

The trap.

Numeric codes do not turn ordinal data into interval data. The numbers 1 to 4 are labels for ordered tiers.

Question 14.Interpret · Regression line

A fitted line is: Revenue (RM’000) = 40 + 2.5 × Marketing spend (RM’000). Which interpretation is correct?

  • A Each extra RM1,000 of spend goes with RM2,500 more revenue on average; at RM10,000, RM65,000 ✓ correct
  • B Each extra RM1,000 of marketing spend causes exactly RM2,500 more revenue in every single firm
  • C Revenue is always RM40,000 plus 2.5% of whatever the firm spends on marketing activities
  • D At RM10,000 of marketing spend, the predicted revenue from the line is RM25,000 for the firm

Correct answer A: Each extra RM1,000 of spend goes with RM2,500 more revenue on average; at RM10,000, RM65,000.

Why this is right.

In Y = a + bX, Y is the outcome (revenue), X the predictor (marketing spend), a (the intercept) the predicted Y when X = 0 and b (the slope) the average change in Y for a one-unit rise in X. Here a = 40 and b = 2.5, so at X = 10 the prediction is 40 + 2.5 × 10 = 65 (RM65,000). “Associated with” is deliberate: the line describes an average pattern, not a guaranteed causal effect on each firm.

The trap.

Dropping the intercept (2.5 × 10 = 25) is the usual arithmetic slip. The intercept is part of every prediction.

Question 15.Interpret · Statistical vs practical significance

In a sample of 1,000 employees, the correlation between hours of AI training and productivity is r = 0.15 with p < .001. How should this be described?

  • A Statistically significant but weak: it explains only about 2% of the variance (0.15² ≈ 0.023) ✓ correct
  • B Strong, because p < .001 shows that the size of the effect must be very large indeed
  • C Moderate, because it explains about 15% of the variance in the productivity scores
  • D Not significant, because the correlation is below the conventional cut-off value of 0.30

Correct answer A: Statistically significant but weak: it explains only about 2% of the variance (0.15² ≈ 0.023).

Why this is right.

A p-value answers “could this be chance?”, not “how big is it?”. With n = 1,000, even a tiny correlation is highly significant. Squaring r gives the shared variance: 0.15² = 0.0225, about 2%. Report both significance and effect size, and judge practical importance by cost and context.

The trap.

A very small p does not mean a very large effect; sample size drives p as much as effect size does.

Question 16.Interpret · Small samples

In a pilot with n = 10 managers, r = 0.45 between digital readiness and performance, but p = 0.19. What is the most reasonable reading?

  • A A significant result, because a correlation of 0.45 is greater than the usual cut-off of 0.30
  • B Proof that there is no relationship between digital readiness and performance in the population
  • C A moderate sample correlation that cannot be told apart from chance; the study is underpowered ✓ correct
  • D An error, because a p-value above .05 can only occur when the correlation is below 0.20

Correct answer C: A moderate sample correlation that cannot be told apart from chance; the study is underpowered.

Why this is right.

With only 10 cases the sampling variability of r is huge: the 95% confidence interval for r = 0.45 here stretches from roughly −0.25 to +0.84. A non-significant result in a tiny sample is inconclusive, not evidence of no effect. The remedy is a larger sample chosen by power analysis, not abandoning the idea.

The trap.

“Not significant” is not “no effect”. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

Question 17.Interpret · Reading a boxplot

A boxplot of customer spend shows the median line close to the bottom of the box and a long upper whisker with several dots above it. What does it suggest?

  • A A bimodal distribution with two distinct groups of customers
  • B A symmetric distribution with unusually high variability overall
  • C A right-skewed distribution with some high-spending outliers ✓ correct
  • D A left-skewed distribution with some very low-spending outliers

Correct answer C: A right-skewed distribution with some high-spending outliers.

Why this is right.

In a boxplot the box runs from Q1 to Q3 and the line marks the median. A median near Q1 means the lower half of the data is compressed, while the long upper whisker and dots show values stretched far to the right: positive skew with high outliers. This is typical of spend data: many modest customers and a few big ones.

The trap.

A boxplot cannot show bimodality because it hides the shape inside the box. Use a histogram or density plot to check for two peaks.

Question 18.Interpret · Robust measures of spread

One day of +7% is added to 99 otherwise quiet days of gold returns (all within ±1%). Which measure of spread changes least?

  • A The standard deviation, because it is expressed in the same units as the returns
  • B The interquartile range, because it depends only on the middle 50% of the data ✓ correct
  • C The variance, because squaring the deviations reduces the influence of large values
  • D The range, because it is based on the two extreme values in the whole data set

Correct answer B: The interquartile range, because it depends only on the middle 50% of the data.

Why this is right.

The range uses the extremes, so it jumps with the shock. Variance and SD square each deviation from the mean, which magnifies big deviations, so a 7% day inflates them sharply. The IQR is the distance between the 25th and 75th percentiles; the shock day sits beyond both and barely moves them.

The trap.

Squaring makes large deviations count more, not less. That is why SD-based risk measures react strongly to crashes.

Quiz 3 — Inferential Tests · Intermediate · 17 questions

Question 1.Interpret · What a p-value means

A study of two teaching methods reports p = 0.03. Which interpretation is correct?

  • A There is a 97% probability that the new teaching method is better than the old method
  • B If there were truly no difference, data this extreme (or more) would turn up in about 3% of studies ✓ correct
  • C There is a 3% probability that the null hypothesis is true, given the data that were collected
  • D The difference between the two teaching methods is 3 marks on average in the exam

Correct answer B: If there were truly no difference, data this extreme (or more) would turn up in about 3% of studies.

Why this is right.

The p-value is a conditional probability: the chance of seeing results at least as extreme as yours, assuming the null hypothesis is true. It is not the probability that the hypothesis is true, and not a measure of effect size. A small p makes the null look implausible, but how plausible the alternative is depends on prior evidence and study quality.

The trap.

“3% chance the null is true” is the most common misreading, because it swaps “data given the hypothesis” with “hypothesis given the data”.

Question 2.Concept · Type I and Type II errors

A company trials an AI tool on 12 employees, finds p = 0.20 and concludes “the tool has no effect on productivity”. What is the main problem?

  • A A non-significant result from a small sample is inconclusive; the test may lack power ✓ correct
  • B The sample is too small for a p-value to be calculated at all, so the result is meaningless
  • C The p-value should have been below 0.50 for a conclusion of this kind to be statistically valid
  • D A p-value of 0.20 proves that the effect is exactly zero in the population of employees

Correct answer A: A non-significant result from a small sample is inconclusive; the test may lack power.

Why this is right.

Failing to reject H0 is not accepting H0. With n = 12 the test could detect only a very large effect. Statistical power is the chance of detecting a true effect of a given size, and small samples have little of it. Report the effect size with a confidence interval, and plan the next study with enough participants.

The trap.

“Not significant” does not mean “no effect”. It means this study could not tell.

Question 3.Calculate · Multiple testing

A researcher tests 20 independent outcomes at α = 0.05 although none truly differs between the groups. What is the probability of at least one false positive?

  • A About 20%
  • B About 5%
  • C About 64% ✓ correct
  • D About 95%

Correct answer C: About 64%.

Why this is right.

Each test has a 95% chance of correctly showing nothing, so the chance of no false positives in 20 independent tests is 0.95²⁰ ≈ 0.36 (α = significance level). Hence P(at least one) = 1 − 0.36 ≈ 0.64. Expect about 20 × 0.05 = 1 false positive on average. Corrections such as Bonferroni (α ÷ number of tests) or false-discovery-rate control keep the family-wise error near 5%.

The trap.

5% applies to a single test. The more tests you run, the likelier it is that something looks “significant” by luck, which is the engine of p-hacking.

Question 4.Apply · Paired data

Productivity is measured for the same 40 employees before and after an AI-training programme. Which test should compare the two means?

  • A A paired-samples t-test, because each employee provides two linked measurements ✓ correct
  • B A one-way ANOVA, because there are two groups of productivity scores to be compared
  • C An independent-samples t-test, because the data come from two different points in time
  • D A chi-square test, because the two groups are labelled “before” and “after” training

Correct answer A: A paired-samples t-test, because each employee provides two linked measurements.

Why this is right.

Paired data have a natural link between each pair of observations (same person, same firm, matched pairs). The paired t-test analyses the 40 difference scores, removing stable person-to-person differences, so it is more powerful than an independent test. A one-way ANOVA with two groups would match an independent t-test but still ignores the pairing.

The trap.

“Two time points” is not “two groups of different people”. Always ask whether the observations are independent.

Question 5.Apply · Unequal variances

You compare mean revenue of Malaysian and Singaporean SMEs (n = 45 and n = 120). Levene’s test is significant (p = .002), showing unequal variances. What should you do?

  • A Use a chi-square test instead, because the variances in the two groups are unequal
  • B Use the standard Student t-test, because t-tests are robust to every violation of assumptions
  • C Read the “equal variances not assumed” row (Welch’s t-test), which adjusts the degrees of freedom ✓ correct
  • D Convert the analysis to a paired-samples t-test to remove the difference in variances

Correct answer C: Read the “equal variances not assumed” row (Welch’s t-test), which adjusts the degrees of freedom.

Why this is right.

Student’s t-test assumes both groups share the same population variance. When that is doubtful, especially with unequal group sizes, the false-positive rate drifts away from 5%. Welch’s test does not assume equal variances and corrects the degrees of freedom; SPSS prints it as “Equal variances not assumed”. Many statisticians now use it routinely.

The trap.

Unequal n combined with unequal variances is the combination that damages the Student t-test most.

Question 6.Interpret · ANOVA result

Satisfaction is compared across four restaurant types (n = 200 in total). ANOVA gives F(3, 196) = 5.2, p = .002. What can you conclude?

  • A Satisfaction depends on restaurant type with 99.8% certainty, since p equals .002 in this test
  • B All four restaurant types differ significantly from each other in their mean satisfaction scores
  • C Fast food differs from fine dining, because the F-test always compares the two extreme groups
  • D At least one restaurant type has a different mean; a post-hoc test is needed to see which ✓ correct

Correct answer D: At least one restaurant type has a different mean; a post-hoc test is needed to see which.

Why this is right.

ANOVA’s null hypothesis is that all population means are equal. A significant F rejects that overall claim but does not say which pairs differ. The degrees of freedom show 4 groups (4 − 1 = 3) and 200 respondents (200 − 4 = 196). Follow up with a post-hoc test such as Tukey’s HSD, and report the effect size η² (eta-squared, the share of total variance explained by group membership).

The trap.

Significance says “not all equal”, not “all different”. And p = .002 is not “99.8% certainty”.

Question 7.Concept · Why post-hoc tests

Why use Tukey’s HSD after ANOVA instead of running all six pairwise t-tests at α = .05?

  • A Pairwise t-tests cannot be calculated at all once the overall ANOVA result has been significant
  • B Six separate tests push the chance of at least one false positive to about 26%; Tukey keeps it near 5% ✓ correct
  • C Tukey’s HSD has more power than a single t-test because it ignores the variance in the groups
  • D Pairwise t-tests are invalid for groups with more than thirty members in each of the groups

Correct answer B: Six separate tests push the chance of at least one false positive to about 26%; Tukey keeps it near 5%.

Why this is right.

With four groups there are 4 × 3 ÷ 2 = 6 pairwise comparisons. The chance of at least one false positive is about 1 − 0.95⁶ ≈ 26% (an approximation, as the tests overlap). Tukey’s HSD (honestly significant difference) adjusts the critical value so that the overall false-positive rate stays at the nominal 5%.

The trap.

Controlling the error across the family of tests costs some power per comparison, which is an acceptable price for credibility.

Question 8.Interpret · Effect size

With n = 4,000 respondents, a t-test comparing two customer segments gives p < .001, but Cohen’s d = 0.08. How should the finding be described?

  • A Not statistically significant, because the value of Cohen’s d is below the cut-off of 0.20
  • B Significant but practically trivial: the groups differ by under a tenth of a standard deviation ✓ correct
  • C Highly meaningful, because p < .001 is much stronger evidence than the usual p < .05
  • D Evidence of a large effect, because the sample size is so big that any difference is important

Correct answer B: Significant but practically trivial: the groups differ by under a tenth of a standard deviation.

Why this is right.

Large samples detect minuscule differences. Cohen’s d expresses the mean difference in SD units; conventional benchmarks are 0.2 small, 0.5 medium and 0.8 large, so d = 0.08 is below “small”. Managers should ask whether a difference of that size justifies any action, and report d alongside p.

The trap.

p tells you how surprising the data are under H0; d tells you how big the difference is. They answer different questions.

Question 9.Calculate · Cohen’s d

Students taught with AI-assisted tutorials average 78 on the exam, those taught traditionally average 72, and the pooled SD is 12. What is Cohen’s d and its conventional label?

  • A d = 6.0, a very large effect
  • B d = 0.50, a small effect
  • C d = 0.08, a negligible effect
  • D d = 0.50, a medium effect ✓ correct

Correct answer D: d = 0.50, a medium effect.

Why this is right.

Cohen’s d = (M1 − M2) ÷ SDpooled, where M1 and M2 are the two group means and SDpooled is their combined standard deviation. Here (78 − 72) ÷ 12 = 0.50. With Cohen’s benchmarks (0.2 small, 0.5 medium, 0.8 large) this is a medium effect: the average AI-taught student scores half an SD higher.

The trap.

The raw difference (6 marks) is meaningful only relative to the spread. Dividing by the SD is what makes effects comparable across studies.

Question 10.Interpret · Confidence interval

A 95% confidence interval for the difference in mean exam scores (AI group minus traditional group) is [1.2, 8.8]. Which statement is supported?

  • A 95% of the students in the AI group scored between 1.2 and 8.8 marks higher than the other group
  • B The difference is not significant, because an interval this wide shows that the data are unreliable
  • C The average score of the AI group lies somewhere between the values of 1.2 and 8.8 marks
  • D Zero is outside the interval, so p < .05; plausible differences run from about 1 to 9 marks ✓ correct

Correct answer D: Zero is outside the interval, so p < .05; plausible differences run from about 1 to 9 marks.

Why this is right.

A confidence interval gives a range of plausible values for a population parameter, here the true mean difference. If a 95% CI for a difference excludes 0, the matching two-sided test at α = .05 (α is the significance level) rejects “no difference”. The width tells you about precision: the data are compatible with a modest 1-mark gain and with a substantial 9-mark gain.

The trap.

A CI is about the population mean difference, not about the spread of individual students.

Question 11.Interpret · Chi-square result

A chi-square test of independence gives χ²(3) = 9.1, p = .028 for gender × payment app. What does this tell you, and what does it not?

  • A An association is indicated, but not its strength or direction; compute Cramér’s V and inspect residuals ✓ correct
  • B That gender causes the choice of payment app, because the association is statistically significant
  • C That the association is strong, because the chi-square statistic itself is larger than the value of 9
  • D That 2.8% of customers choose a different payment app from the one that was expected for them

Correct answer A: An association is indicated, but not its strength or direction; compute Cramér’s V and inspect residuals.

Why this is right.

Chi-square tests whether the two categorical variables are independent. A significant χ² says the pattern differs from what independence predicts, not how strongly. Effect size: Cramér’s V = √(χ² ÷ (n × (k − 1))), where n is the sample size and k the smaller of the number of rows and columns. Adjusted standardised residuals show which cells drive the result.

The trap.

χ² grows with n, so it is not an effect size; V is.

Question 12.Apply · Non-parametric choice

Satisfaction ratings (1–5) from two independent groups of 14 customers each are strongly skewed. Which test is most appropriate?

  • A Mann–Whitney U: the outcome is ordinal and skewed, and the groups are small and independent ✓ correct
  • B A paired-samples t-test, because there are two groups of customers that must be compared
  • C Pearson’s correlation, because there are two sets of ratings that have to be related to each other
  • D The Wilcoxon signed-rank test, because the data consist of ranks rather than raw measurements

Correct answer A: Mann–Whitney U: the outcome is ordinal and skewed, and the groups are small and independent.

Why this is right.

With small n and clear non-normality, the CLT cannot be relied on, so a rank-based test is safer. Mann–Whitney U (equivalent to the Wilcoxon rank-sum test) compares two independent groups using ranks. Wilcoxon signed-rank is its paired counterpart; Kruskal–Wallis handles three or more independent groups.

The trap.

Ranks being involved is not enough to choose Wilcoxon signed-rank. The key question is whether the observations are paired.

Question 13.Apply · Repeated measures

The same 30 students sit a statistics test in week 1, week 5 and week 10. Which analysis fits best?

  • A One-way independent ANOVA, because there are three different time points to be compared
  • B A chi-square test of independence between the week of the test and the score obtained
  • C Repeated-measures ANOVA, because the same 30 students provide all three sets of scores ✓ correct
  • D Three independent-samples t-tests, one for each pair of the testing weeks in the study

Correct answer C: Repeated-measures ANOVA, because the same 30 students provide all three sets of scores.

Why this is right.

The three sets of scores come from the same people, so they are correlated. Repeated-measures ANOVA accounts for this within-person dependence. An independent ANOVA would treat 90 scores as 90 people, which is wrong, and three separate t-tests would raise the false-positive risk.

The trap.

Treating repeated observations as independent inflates the sample size and invalidates the p-values.

Question 14.Interpret · Multiple regression coefficient

In a multiple regression predicting SME revenue (RM’000), the unstandardised coefficient for marketing spend (RM’000) is 0.42, with employees and years in operation also in the model. How should it be interpreted?

  • A Marketing spend explains 42% of the variance in revenue once the other two predictors are included
  • B Each extra RM1,000 of marketing spend raises revenue by RM42,000 in every SME without exception
  • C The coefficient is the correlation between marketing spend and revenue, ignoring the other predictors
  • D Holding the other predictors constant, each extra RM1,000 of spend goes with RM420 more revenue ✓ correct

Correct answer D: Holding the other predictors constant, each extra RM1,000 of spend goes with RM420 more revenue.

Why this is right.

A multiple-regression slope is the expected change in the outcome for a one-unit rise in that predictor with the other predictors held constant. Units matter: both variables are in RM thousand, so 0.42 means RM0.42 of revenue per RM1 of marketing, or RM420 per RM1,000. “Held constant” is what makes regression more informative than separate correlations.

The trap.

A raw coefficient is not a correlation and not a percentage of variance (that is R²).

Question 15.Diagnose · Multicollinearity

In a regression, two predictors, monthly ad spend and social-media spend, correlate at r = .93 and each has a VIF of about 8.5. What is the main consequence?

  • A The residuals of the model will become non-normal, so every test in the output is unusable
  • B The effects of the two predictors are exactly cancelled out, so the model predicts nothing at all
  • C Coefficients become unstable and imprecise, so each predictor can look insignificant ✓ correct
  • D The model’s R² is invalid and has to be recalculated after one of the two predictors is dropped

Correct answer C: Coefficients become unstable and imprecise, so each predictor can look insignificant.

Why this is right.

VIF (variance inflation factor) = 1 ÷ (1 − R²j), where R²j is the R² from regressing predictor j on all the other predictors. A VIF of 8.5 means the estimate for that predictor is far less precise than if it were uncorrelated with the others. Common cut-offs are 5 (conservative) and 10. Remedies: combine the predictors into one index, drop one, or collect more data.

The trap.

Collinearity affects the individual coefficients and their precision, not overall fit: R² and predictions can remain fine.

Question 16.Concept · One-tailed versus two-tailed

After seeing that the AI group scored higher, a researcher switches from a two-tailed to a one-tailed test, turning p = .08 into p = .04. Why is this inappropriate?

  • A The p-value should have doubled rather than halved when the researcher switched to the other test
  • B The direction needs theoretical justification before the data; switching afterwards just halves p ✓ correct
  • C One-tailed tests are never permitted in management research, whatever the theory predicts
  • D One-tailed tests can only be used when the sample size is above one hundred participants

Correct answer B: The direction needs theoretical justification before the data; switching afterwards just halves p.

Why this is right.

A one-tailed test puts the entire 5% rejection region in one direction, so it is more powerful for that direction but blind to an effect in the opposite one. It is legitimate only when the hypothesis was directional before data collection and an effect in the other direction would be treated as no different from zero. Choosing the tail after peeking is a form of p-hacking.

The trap.

Switching halves the p-value only because the data went the way the researcher saw. That is exactly the peeking that is not allowed.

Question 17.Apply · Choose the analysis

A researcher wants to know whether payment-app preference (GrabPay, Touch ’n Go, PayNow, card) is related to age group (18–30, 31–45, 46+), using 400 customers. Which analysis fits?

  • A A chi-square test of independence on the 4 × 3 table, with Cramér’s V as the effect size ✓ correct
  • B Pearson’s correlation, because both variables become numeric once they have been coded
  • C A one-way ANOVA, because there are three age groups whose means have to be compared
  • D A paired-samples t-test, because each customer reports both of the variables in the study

Correct answer A: A chi-square test of independence on the 4 × 3 table, with Cramér’s V as the effect size.

Why this is right.

Both variables are categorical (a nominal payment app and ordered but grouped age bands), and the question concerns association. The chi-square test of independence compares observed cell counts with those expected if the variables were unrelated. Pearson’s r and ANOVA need a numeric outcome; paired t-tests need two numeric measurements on the same cases.

The trap.

Coding categories as 1, 2, 3 does not make them numeric measures. Choose the test from the measurement level, not from the data file.

Quiz 4 — SEM Foundations · Intermediate · 16 questions

Question 1.Concept · Why SEM

Why might a researcher prefer structural equation modelling (SEM) to ordinary regression on summed scores when studying customer satisfaction, trust and loyalty?

  • A SEM works only with observed variables, which is the reason why it is considered more accurate
  • B SEM removes the need for any theory, because the software discovers the relationships by itself
  • C SEM treats constructs as latent and allows for measurement error in their indicators ✓ correct
  • D SEM guarantees causal conclusions, because it draws arrows between the variables in the model

Correct answer C: SEM treats constructs as latent and allows for measurement error in their indicators.

Why this is right.

Regression on summed scores treats each score as if it were measured perfectly. SEM separates the latent construct from the error in each indicator and estimates the measurement and structural parts together. This gives less biased relationships when constructs are measured by imperfect survey items, which is nearly always the case in management research.

The trap.

The arrows in SEM encode theory that the researcher supplies. They are not proof of cause.

Question 2.Concept · Latent variables

Which of the following is best treated as a latent variable in a model of SME performance?

  • A Employee engagement, inferred from responses to several survey items ✓ correct
  • B Years in operation, as calculated from the firm’s date of registration
  • C The number of employees, as recorded in the firm’s human-resources system
  • D Annual revenue, as taken from the firm’s audited financial statements

Correct answer A: Employee engagement, inferred from responses to several survey items.

Why this is right.

A latent variable (construct) cannot be observed directly; it is inferred from observable indicators such as questionnaire items. Engagement, trust and digital readiness are typical latent constructs. Headcount, audited revenue and years in operation are recorded directly, so they are observed variables.

The trap.

A variable being abstract is not enough. The test is whether it must be measured indirectly, through indicators, because it cannot be read off a record.

Question 3.Diagnose · Reflective or formative

Which pairing of construct and measurement model is correct?

  • A Both constructs are reflective, because any survey items always reflect an underlying idea in the minds of the respondents
  • B Customer satisfaction measured by “I am pleased”, “I am happy” and “I would recommend” is reflective; digital readiness measured by IT budget, staff skills, cloud adoption and cybersecurity policy is formative ✓ correct
  • C Customer satisfaction measured by “I am pleased”, “I am happy” and “I would recommend” is formative; digital readiness measured by IT budget, staff skills, cloud adoption and cybersecurity policy is reflective
  • D Both constructs are formative, because the indicators of a construct always combine to create that construct

Correct answer B: Customer satisfaction measured by “I am pleased”, “I am happy” and “I would recommend” is reflective; digital readiness measured by IT budget, staff skills, cloud adoption and cybersecurity policy is formative.

Why this is right.

Ask which way the causality runs. Satisfaction causes people to say they are pleased and happy: the indicators are interchangeable manifestations, they should correlate highly, and removing one does not change the meaning (reflective). Digital readiness is made up of its components: a larger IT budget does not imply more staff skill; the indicators are not interchangeable, need not correlate, and dropping one changes what the construct means (formative).

The trap.

Do not decide from the wording of the questionnaire items. Decide from the conceptual relationship between the construct and its indicators.

Question 4.Identify · Exogenous and endogenous

A model has these paths: Management Support → Digital Readiness; Digital Readiness → Business Performance; Competitive Pressure → Business Performance. Which constructs are endogenous?

  • A Digital Readiness only
  • B Digital Readiness and Business Performance ✓ correct
  • C Management Support and Competitive Pressure
  • D Business Performance only

Correct answer B: Digital Readiness and Business Performance.

Why this is right.

An endogenous construct has at least one arrow pointing into it: it is explained by other constructs in the model. Management Support and Competitive Pressure have only outgoing arrows (exogenous). Digital Readiness receives an arrow from Management Support and sends one to Performance, so it is endogenous and also a potential mediator.

The trap.

A construct that both receives and sends arrows is still endogenous. Being a predictor for one relationship does not make it exogenous.

Question 5.Interpret · Path coefficient

In a PLS-SEM model the standardised path coefficient from Digital Capability to Business Performance is 0.35 (bootstrapped t = 4.8). What does 0.35 mean?

  • A Business Performance increases by 35% whenever Digital Capability increases by a full 35% as well
  • B Digital Capability explains 35% of the variance in Business Performance across all of the SMEs studied
  • C A 1-SD rise in Digital Capability goes with a 0.35-SD rise in Performance, other paths held constant ✓ correct
  • D There is a 35% probability that the hypothesis about this relationship is in fact true or correct

Correct answer C: A 1-SD rise in Digital Capability goes with a 0.35-SD rise in Performance, other paths held constant.

Why this is right.

Standardised path coefficients are read like standardised regression coefficients (betas) and typically fall between −1 and +1. Explained variance is a separate quantity (R²), reported for each endogenous construct. A bootstrapped t of 4.8 shows that the path differs significantly from zero.

The trap.

Path coefficient, R² and p-value answer three different questions: how strong, how much is explained, and how reliable.

Question 6.Apply · PLS-SEM or CB-SEM

A study has n = 85 SMEs, non-normal data, a model with one formative construct, and the aim of predicting performance. Which approach is best suited?

  • A PLS-SEM: suits small samples, non-normal data, formative constructs and prediction ✓ correct
  • B CB-SEM, because it is always the preferred method for doctoral research in management disciplines
  • C CB-SEM, because it makes far fewer assumptions about the distribution of the observed data
  • D Ordinary regression, because SEM cannot be used when the sample is fewer than one hundred cases

Correct answer A: PLS-SEM: suits small samples, non-normal data, formative constructs and prediction.

Why this is right.

CB-SEM suits theory confirmation with larger samples, multivariate-normal data and reflective constructs, and offers global fit indices. PLS-SEM is a variance-based method suited to prediction, complex models and formative measures; it makes no distributional assumptions and runs with small samples (though statistical power still matters).

The trap.

Neither method is “better” in general. Each fits a different purpose.

Question 7.Apply · CB-SEM choice

A researcher wants to confirm a well-established four-factor scale on 600 respondents with roughly normal data and reflective indicators, and to report global model fit indices. Which approach fits?

  • A CB-SEM (for example AMOS or lavaan), because the aim is confirmation and global model fit ✓ correct
  • B PLS-SEM, because it is the only SEM method that can handle a sample of 600 respondents
  • C PLS-SEM, because it always produces the same global fit indices as CB-SEM does
  • D Exploratory factor analysis, because the factors of the scale are already well established

Correct answer A: CB-SEM (for example AMOS or lavaan), because the aim is confirmation and global model fit.

Why this is right.

CB-SEM estimates parameters to reproduce the observed covariance matrix, which is why it supplies chi-square based fit indices such as CFI (comparative fit index) and RMSEA (root mean square error of approximation). With a confirmatory aim, a large sample, normal data and reflective items, it is the natural choice. Exploratory factor analysis discovers structure; it does not confirm it.

The trap.

Confirming an established factor structure is confirmatory factor analysis, not exploratory.

Question 8.Concept · Sample size rules

Why is the “10-times rule” (ten cases per arrow pointing at the most complex construct) not recommended as the sole basis for PLS-SEM sample size?

  • A It always overestimates the sample size, which wastes time and resources in a study
  • B It ignores effect size and power, so it may give samples too small for realistic effects ✓ correct
  • C It was designed for CB-SEM only, so it is invalid for any model estimated with PLS-SEM
  • D It requires the data to be normally distributed, which PLS-SEM is unable to provide

Correct answer B: It ignores effect size and power, so it may give samples too small for realistic effects.

Why this is right.

Rules of thumb are convenient but blind to how large the effects are. Hair et al. recommend power analysis or the inverse-square-root method, which links the minimum sample size to the smallest path coefficient you want to detect. Smaller expected paths require larger samples. A hard cap on observations in a student licence is a practical reason to keep models simple.

The trap.

The sample must be large enough for the effect you hope to detect, not for the number of arrows.

Question 9.Concept · Bootstrapping

Why does PLS-SEM use bootstrapping to test whether path coefficients are significant?

  • A It is required only for formative constructs and is never used with reflective constructs
  • B It converts the data to a normal distribution so that ordinary t-tests become valid
  • C No distribution is assumed, so significance comes from resampling the data repeatedly ✓ correct
  • D Bootstrapping increases the sample size by collecting additional respondents for the study

Correct answer C: No distribution is assumed, so significance comes from resampling the data repeatedly.

Why this is right.

PLS-SEM is non-parametric: there is no simple formula for the precision of each estimate. Bootstrapping draws many random subsamples with replacement from the original data, re-estimates the model each time, and uses the spread of the estimates to produce t-values, p-values and confidence intervals. It adds no information beyond your data; it only gauges the stability of the estimates.

The trap.

Resampling does not create new participants. A small, biased sample stays small and biased.

Question 10.Calculate · Bootstrap t-value

After 5,000 bootstrap samples, a path has t = 1.70. At the 5% significance level (two-tailed), what is the correct statement?

  • A It cannot be assessed without also knowing the size of the path coefficient
  • B Significant at 1%, because a t-value of 1.70 is greater than the value of 1.65
  • C Not significant at 5% (critical t ≈ 1.96); only significant at the 10% level ✓ correct
  • D Significant at 5%, because a t-value of 1.70 is comfortably above a value of 1.0

Correct answer C: Not significant at 5% (critical t ≈ 1.96); only significant at the 10% level.

Why this is right.

Two-tailed critical values for large samples are about 1.65 (10%), 1.96 (5%) and 2.58 (1%). A t of 1.70 clears 1.65 but not 1.96, so it is significant at the 10% level only. Report the exact p-value and the bootstrap confidence interval; if the interval includes zero, the path is not significant.

The trap.

Mixing one-tailed and two-tailed critical values is a common slip. 1.65 is the two-tailed 10% value (or the one-tailed 5% value).

Question 11.Interpret · R²

R² for “Customer Loyalty” is 0.62 in a PLS-SEM model. What does it mean?

  • A The path coefficients leading to Loyalty add up to a total value of 0.62
  • B Loyalty is measured with 62% accuracy for each individual customer in the sample
  • C There is a 62% chance that the hypotheses in the model are supported by the data
  • D The predictors together explain 62% of the variance in Loyalty in this sample ✓ correct

Correct answer D: The predictors together explain 62% of the variance in Loyalty in this sample.

Why this is right.

R² is the proportion of variance of an endogenous construct accounted for by the constructs pointing to it. Judge it against the field: in consumer-behaviour studies Hair et al. treat 0.75 as substantial, 0.50 as moderate and 0.25 as weak (Cohen’s regression benchmarks of 0.26, 0.13 and 0.02 are more lenient). It is an in-sample measure, so it does not demonstrate predictive power for new data.

The trap.

R² is explanatory (in-sample), not predictive. Prediction for new cases needs a separate check.

Question 12.Concept · Causal claims

A student writes that PLS-SEM “proves” digital capability causes higher business performance. How should the claim be corrected?

  • A The claim is correct, because bootstrapping with 5,000 samples removes any possible confounding
  • B The claim is correct, because the arrows in an SEM diagram always represent genuine causes in reality
  • C The claim is wrong, because SEM can only describe the correlations between the observed items
  • D PLS-SEM estimates relationships consistent with a theory; causal claims also need design and timing ✓ correct

Correct answer D: PLS-SEM estimates relationships consistent with a theory; causal claims also need design and timing.

Why this is right.

Arrows state the researcher’s theory; the software estimates the strength of those relationships under assumptions. A cross-sectional survey cannot establish temporal precedence or rule out omitted variables. Use “is positively associated with” and discuss causal inference in the limitations, or use longitudinal or experimental data.

The trap.

Resampling cannot fix confounding. Only design and measurement can.

Question 13.Concept · Outer and inner model

In a PLS-SEM path diagram, which description of the outer (measurement) model and the inner (structural) model is correct?

  • A The outer model links the constructs to each other; the inner model links each construct to its indicators
  • B The outer model describes the survey sample; the inner model describes the software settings that are used
  • C The outer model contains only the dependent constructs; the inner model contains only the independent ones
  • D The outer model links constructs to their indicators; the inner model links constructs to each other ✓ correct

Correct answer D: The outer model links constructs to their indicators; the inner model links constructs to each other.

Why this is right.

The measurement (outer) model specifies how each latent construct is measured by its indicators, reflective or formative. The structural (inner) model specifies the hypothesised paths among the constructs. Hypotheses are tested on the inner model.

The trap.

Do not mix up the names: “outer” is the ring of indicators around each construct; “inner” is the web of paths between constructs.

Question 14.Interpret · Is the hypothesis supported?

Hypothesis H1 predicts a positive path from Digital Capability to Performance. The bootstrapped result is β = 0.12, t = 1.2, p = .23. What is the correct conclusion?

  • A H1 is partly supported, because a t-value of 1.2 is above zero and so shows a helpful trend
  • B H1 is not supported: the path is positive in sign but cannot be distinguished from zero ✓ correct
  • C H1 is rejected, because a p-value above .05 proves that Digital Capability has no effect at all
  • D H1 is supported, because the path coefficient has the positive sign that the hypothesis predicted

Correct answer B: H1 is not supported: the path is positive in sign but cannot be distinguished from zero.

Why this is right.

A hypothesis is supported only when the path has the predicted sign and is statistically significant (here p < .05, or |t| above about 1.96). With p = .23 the estimate is compatible with no effect. Report it as not supported; you may discuss low power or a small effect, but you cannot claim support.

The trap.

“Not supported” is not “disproved”. A non-significant path does not prove the effect is zero; it means this sample cannot show it.

Question 15.Interpret · Significant but in the wrong direction

H2 predicts a positive path from Competitive Pressure to Digital Adoption. The result is β = −0.31, t = 4.0, p < .001. What should the researcher conclude?

  • A H2 is not supported: the path is significant but in the opposite direction ✓ correct
  • B The result is invalid, because a path coefficient can never be negative in a PLS-SEM model
  • C H2 is supported, because it is the size of the coefficient that matters and not its sign
  • D H2 is supported, because the path is highly significant and has a large t-value

Correct answer A: H2 is not supported: the path is significant but in the opposite direction.

Why this is right.

A directional hypothesis needs both the predicted sign and significance. A strong negative path contradicts the prediction, which is a finding, not an error. Possible explanations include an omitted variable, context (firms under pressure may be cutting costs rather than investing) or flawed theory. Report it as unsupported and discuss why.

The trap.

Significance alone is not support. The direction has to match the hypothesis.

Question 16.Diagnose · Model size against sample size

A student plans a model with 8 constructs and 40 indicators using 60 SME responses. What is the main risk?

  • A The model will be too simple, because PLS-SEM cannot estimate more than five constructs
  • B None, because PLS-SEM works with any sample size however small the sample happens to be
  • C Bootstrapping will correct the problem by creating extra respondents from the existing sample
  • D Too little data for so complex a model: unstable estimates and missed real effects ✓ correct

Correct answer D: Too little data for so complex a model: unstable estimates and missed real effects.

Why this is right.

PLS-SEM tolerates smaller samples than CB-SEM, but it is not free of limits: more constructs, indicators and paths mean more parameters to estimate from the same data, which lowers statistical power and makes estimates unstable. Options: simplify the model, collect more data, or split the study into two.

The trap.

Resampling does not create new respondents. Bootstrapping measures how stable your estimates are; it cannot repair a thin sample.

Question 1.Identify · Mediation or moderation

Statement 1: “AI tutoring improves exam performance because it first raises student engagement.” Statement 2: “AI tutoring improves performance more strongly for students with high digital literacy.” Which classification is correct?

  • A Both statements describe moderators, because each variable changes the effect of tutoring
  • B Statement 1: engagement is a mediator; Statement 2: digital literacy is a moderator ✓ correct
  • C Both statements describe mediators, because each variable explains the effect of tutoring
  • D Statement 1: engagement is a moderator; Statement 2: digital literacy is a mediator

Correct answer B: Statement 1: engagement is a mediator; Statement 2: digital literacy is a moderator.

Why this is right.

A mediator sits in the causal chain and explains how or why X affects Y (X → M → Y). A moderator changes the strength or direction of the X → Y relationship and answers “for whom” or “when”; it does not transmit the effect. Quick test: “because” or “through” signals mediation; “stronger for” or “depends on” signals moderation.

The trap.

The same variable can be a mediator in one theory and a moderator in another. Classify by its role in the hypothesis, not by its name.

Question 2.Calculate · Indirect and total effects

In a mediation model, the path from Management Support to Digital Readiness is a = 0.50 and from Digital Readiness to Performance is b = 0.40. The direct path from Management Support to Performance is c′ = 0.15. What are the indirect and total effects?

  • A Indirect = 0.90; total = 1.05
  • B Indirect = 0.10; total = 0.25
  • C Indirect = 0.20; total = 0.20
  • D Indirect = 0.20; total = 0.35 ✓ correct

Correct answer D: Indirect = 0.20; total = 0.35.

Why this is right.

The indirect effect is the product of the paths along the mediated route: a × b = 0.50 × 0.40 = 0.20, where a is the X → M path and b the M → Y path. The total effect is the indirect effect plus the direct effect: 0.20 + 0.15 = 0.35. Paths multiply along a route and add across routes.

The trap.

Adding the paths (0.50 + 0.40) is wrong: effects along a chain multiply.

Question 3.Interpret · Type of mediation

The bootstrapped indirect effect has a 95% CI of [0.08, 0.31], while the direct effect is 0.04 (p = .62). What kind of mediation is supported?

  • A Full (indirect-only) mediation: the effect runs through the mediator, not directly ✓ correct
  • B No mediation at all, because the direct effect from the predictor is not significant
  • C Moderation, because there are two separate effects in the model rather than just one
  • D Partial mediation, because the indirect effect is significant in the bootstrapped results

Correct answer A: Full (indirect-only) mediation: the effect runs through the mediator, not directly.

Before the solution: a two-minute tutorial.

Mediation and moderation both bring in a third variable, so they are easy to mix up. But the third variable does a different job in each. Learn the two jobs and this question becomes easy. A memory trick: a mediator is the middle step. A moderator modifies the strength.

Job one: mediation is a stepping stone.

Mediation answers the question: how, or why, does X affect Y?

Studying lifts your mark. Why? Because studying builds understanding, and understanding lifts the mark. Understanding is the stepping stone in the middle. It is the mediator, M.

To test it, you multiply the two hops, a times b. That gives the indirect effect. Then you use bootstrapping to get a confidence interval. If the interval misses zero, the stepping stone is real.

Job two: moderation is a dial.

Moderation answers a different question: when, or for whom, is the effect of X on Y stronger or weaker?

Studying lifts your mark, but how much depends on how tired you are. A well-rested student gains a lot from each hour of study. A very tired student gains very little. Tiredness is not a step in the chain. It turns the strength of studying up or down, like a volume dial. Tiredness is the moderator, W.

To test it, you add an interaction term, which is X multiplied by W. If the interaction is significant, the dial really does change the effect.

Quick sorting game: mediation or moderation?

  1. Sleeping badly lowers grades because it stops you concentrating. Answer: mediation. Concentration is the stepping stone between sleep and grades.
  2. Advertising lifts sales more in summer than in winter. Answer: moderation. The season is a dial that changes how strong the effect of advertising is.
  3. A pay rise makes staff happier, and happier staff stay longer. Answer: mediation. Happiness is the stepping stone between the pay rise and staying.
  4. Homework helps grades more for students who have a good teacher. Answer: moderation. Teacher quality is a dial.

Now you are ready for the question.

This question gives numbers for the stepping-stone route, which is the indirect effect, and for the shortcut, which is the direct effect. Those are mediation tools. There is no dial and no interaction term, so this is a mediation question. Here is the full solution.

The full solution. Picture it as a school story.

You want a high mark on a maths test. That mark is Y. To get it, you study for five hours. Studying is X.

But studying cannot put marks on the paper by itself. What it really does is help you understand the topic. Understanding is M, the middle step, which we call the mediator.

So the effect of studying travels in two hops: studying lifts understanding, and understanding lifts your mark. Multiply the two hops together and you get the indirect effect.

Now ask a second question. Once understanding is taken care of, does studying still give any extra boost through a secret shortcut? That extra boost is the direct effect.

Key to the letters. X is studying. M is understanding. Y is the test mark. The letter a is how strongly studying lifts understanding. The letter b is how strongly understanding lifts the mark. The indirect effect is a times b. The direct effect, written c prime, is the shortcut from X straight to Y.

Number one: the indirect effect, 0.08 to 0.31.

A bootstrap is a computer trick. It redoes your whole study thousands of times. Each time it draws students from your sample, like pulling names out of a hat and putting each name back afterwards. Every redo gives a slightly different indirect effect.

Line up all those results and keep the middle 95 per cent. They run from 0.08 to 0.31. That range is the 95% confidence interval. It is the set of values the true indirect effect could believably have.

Zero means nothing is happening. Is zero inside the range? No. Even the lowest believable value, 0.08, is above zero. So we are confident the indirect effect is real. In statistics words, it is significant.

Number two: the direct effect, 0.04 with p = .62.

First, 0.04 is tiny. Second, p = .62 works like a luck meter. It says: if the shortcut really did nothing, then in about 62 out of every 100 samples you would still see a number at least this far from zero, just by luck.

Sixty-two in a hundred is very common. So we cannot tell this 0.04 apart from zero. The direct effect is not significant. The usual cut-off for significant is 5 in 100, or p below .05.

Put the two together.

The route through understanding is real. The shortcut is not. So all of the effect of studying travels through understanding. That is full mediation, also called indirect-only mediation. The answer is A.

The trap: answer B.

A non-significant direct effect does not mean “no mediation”. The mediation test is on the indirect effect. Picture a closed shortcut and a busy motorway through the middle town. A closed shortcut does not mean nobody is travelling.

Why C and D are wrong.

Answer C says moderation. Moderation is a different idea. A moderator is like a dial that changes how strong an effect is, for example studying might help older students more than younger ones. A mediator is a step in the chain. Having two effects in a model does not make it moderation.

Question 4.Concept · Testing the indirect effect

Why is a bootstrap confidence interval the preferred way to test an indirect effect?

  • A The product a × b is not normally distributed, so a normal-theory test is inaccurate ✓ correct
  • B It can only be applied when the constructs in the structural model are all formative
  • C Bootstrapping removes the direct effect from the structural model before the indirect one is tested
  • D A normal-theory test needs samples of several thousand respondents before it gives any answer

Correct answer A: The product a × b is not normally distributed, so a normal-theory test is inaccurate.

Why this is right.

Even when a and b (the X → M and M → Y paths) are each approximately normal, their product has a skewed distribution, so a test that assumes normality loses power. A percentile or bias-corrected bootstrap interval for a × b reflects its true shape. If the 95% interval excludes zero, the indirect effect is significant.

The trap.

The bootstrap tests the indirect effect directly, rather than inferring it from two separate significance tests.

Question 5.Interpret · Interaction effect

An interaction term (Marketing Spend × Owner Digital Literacy) has a path coefficient of +0.18 (p = .01) on Revenue. What does this mean?

  • A Marketing spend and digital literacy are strongly correlated with each other in the sample
  • B Digital literacy has a direct effect of 0.18 on revenue, independent of marketing spend
  • C Marketing’s positive effect on revenue grows stronger as owner digital literacy rises ✓ correct
  • D Digital literacy mediates the effect of marketing spend on revenue in the structural model

Correct answer C: Marketing’s positive effect on revenue grows stronger as owner digital literacy rises.

Why this is right.

The interaction coefficient measures how the X → Y slope changes for each unit of the moderator. Positive means the slope grows with the moderator (amplification); negative means it weakens (buffering). Interpret it with simple-slope analysis at, for example, low (−1 SD), average and high (+1 SD) moderator levels.

The trap.

The interaction is a different parameter from the moderator’s own direct effect, which is estimated separately.

Question 6.Interpret · Simple slopes

Simple-slope analysis shows that the effect of marketing on revenue is +0.10 (not significant) at low digital literacy (−1 SD), +0.28 at average and +0.46 at high literacy (+1 SD). What is the best summary?

  • A Marketing pays off at average or high literacy; at low literacy no reliable effect shows ✓ correct
  • B Marketing has exactly the same effect on revenue at every level of owner digital literacy
  • C Digital literacy plays no role at all, because the slope is positive at every one of the levels
  • D Marketing damages revenue when owner digital literacy is low, so it should then be avoided

Correct answer A: Marketing pays off at average or high literacy; at low literacy no reliable effect shows.

Why this is right.

Moderation is conditional: the slope is a function of the moderator. Here it rises from 0.10 to 0.46 across the range, and only the 0.10 slope is not significant. The managerial reading: invest in digital-literacy training alongside marketing, otherwise the money is largely wasted.

The trap.

“Not significant” at low literacy is different from “negative”. The data show no reliable effect, not a harmful one.

Question 7.Concept · Mean-centring

Why are indicators usually mean-centred (or standardised) before an interaction term is created?

  • A It converts the moderator into a mediator, which then lets it carry the effect
  • B It cuts collinearity with its components and makes main effects readable at average levels ✓ correct
  • C It guarantees that the interaction term will turn out to be statistically significant
  • D It transforms the data to a normal distribution so that the bootstrapping becomes valid

Correct answer B: It cuts collinearity with its components and makes main effects readable at average levels.

Why this is right.

The product of two raw variables is strongly correlated with each of them, which makes the estimates unstable. Centring each variable at its mean removes most of that correlation, and makes each main effect represent its effect at the average level of the other variable rather than at zero (which may not exist, for example on a 1–7 scale).

The trap.

Centring cannot manufacture an interaction. It only improves stability and interpretation.

Question 8.Interpret · Predictive relevance Q²

Blindfolding gives Q² = 0.31 for Business Performance. What does this indicate?

  • A No predictive relevance, because the value of Q² is lower than the value of R²
  • B A 31% prediction error rate when the model is used to predict new observations
  • C Weak predictive relevance, because the value of Q² is lower than the value of 0.50
  • D Medium relevance: Q² above 0 means relevance, with about 0.25 medium and 0.50 large ✓ correct

Correct answer D: Medium relevance: Q² above 0 means relevance, with about 0.25 medium and 0.50 large.

Why this is right.

Q² (Stone–Geisser) assesses predictive relevance by omitting parts of the data, predicting them and comparing the errors with a naive benchmark. Q² > 0 means the model predicts better than the benchmark; Hair et al. treat values above 0, 0.25 and 0.50 as small, medium and large predictive relevance.

The trap.

Q² is an index relative to a benchmark, not an error percentage.

Question 9.Diagnose · Overfitting

A model reports R² = 0.71, but in out-of-sample prediction it does no better than simply predicting the sample mean. What is the likely problem?

  • A Multicollinearity among the outcome indicators, which always lowers predictive accuracy
  • B Underfitting: the value of R² is far too low to support any claims about prediction
  • C Overfitting: it fits noise in this sample, so in-sample fit does not transfer to prediction ✓ correct
  • D The measurement model is formative, which makes in-sample and out-of-sample results differ

Correct answer C: Overfitting: it fits noise in this sample, so in-sample fit does not transfer to prediction.

Why this is right.

A model with many paths and parameters can fit the quirks of one sample. In-sample R² rises with every added path, while out-of-sample accuracy may not. Check predictive performance on cases the model has not seen, prefer parsimonious models and be cautious about claims such as “explains 71% of performance”.

The trap.

An R² of 0.71 is high, so underfitting is excluded. The point is that this R² was measured on the same data used to fit the model.

Question 10.Concept · Higher-order constructs

“Digital Capability” is defined by three conceptually distinct dimensions (Infrastructure, Skills and Governance), each measured by three items. Why model it as a higher-order construct?

  • A It expresses an abstract concept through its dimensions and keeps the model parsimonious ✓ correct
  • B Because a higher-order construct forces the three dimensions to be completely uncorrelated
  • C Because higher-order constructs avoid the need for any reliability or validity checks
  • D Because a second-order construct is required for any construct with nine or more items

Correct answer A: It expresses an abstract concept through its dimensions and keeps the model parsimonious.

Why this is right.

A higher-order construct (HOC) captures a general concept formed by, or reflected in, lower-order components (LOCs). Benefits: fewer paths from the dimensions to other constructs, higher bandwidth and theoretical clarity. You must specify the type (reflective-reflective, reflective-formative, formative-reflective or formative-formative) from theory, and assess validity at both levels.

The trap.

The number of items is not the reason. The conceptual structure is.

Question 11.Concept · Control variables

A study of SME digital adoption and revenue includes firm size and firm age as control variables. What is their purpose?

  • A To act as mediators of the main relationship between adoption and revenue
  • B To reduce alternative explanations by accounting for firm size and age ✓ correct
  • C To make the sample more representative of the whole population of SMEs
  • D To increase the number of hypotheses that are being tested in the study

Correct answer B: To reduce alternative explanations by accounting for firm size and age.

Why this is right.

Controls are variables that may affect the outcome but are not the focus of the hypotheses. By including them (typically as predictors of the outcome construct), you estimate the focal effect net of their influence. They do not make a design causal; they only address measured confounders. Include only variables with theoretical justification.

The trap.

Adding every available variable as a “control” can bias results and cost statistical power.

Question 12.Apply · Writing a moderation hypothesis

Which hypothesis states a moderation effect?

  • A Digital readiness improves performance by first increasing operational efficiency in the firm
  • B Larger firms have higher performance than smaller firms, whatever their digital readiness
  • C Digital readiness and firm size are positively correlated with each other across all firms
  • D The positive effect of digital readiness on performance is stronger for larger firms ✓ correct

Correct answer D: The positive effect of digital readiness on performance is stronger for larger firms.

Why this is right.

A moderation hypothesis says the strength or direction of an X → Y relationship depends on a third variable (here firm size). The first alternative is mediation (X → efficiency → Y), the second is a direct effect of size and the third is a correlation. Moderation wording usually contains “stronger for”, “depends on” or “weaker when”.

The trap.

A moderator does not sit between X and Y in a chain. It acts on the arrow itself.

Question 13.Calculate · Opposing direct and indirect effects

In a mediation model, a × b = 0.30 while the direct effect is c′ = −0.12 (both significant). What is the total effect, and what pattern is this?

  • A Total = 0.42; complementary mediation, because both of the effects are significant
  • B Total = 0.18; competitive mediation, as the direct and indirect effects have opposite signs ✓ correct
  • C Total = −0.12; no mediation, because the direct effect has the opposite sign to the indirect
  • D Total = 0.18; full mediation, because the indirect effect is larger than the direct effect

Correct answer B: Total = 0.18; competitive mediation, as the direct and indirect effects have opposite signs.

Why this is right.

The total effect is the indirect effect plus the direct effect: 0.30 + (−0.12) = 0.18 (a × b is the indirect effect through the mediator; c′ is the direct effect). When both are significant but have opposite signs, the mediator transmits one effect while another route works against it (competitive mediation, sometimes called suppression). Looking only at the total effect would understate both mechanisms.

The trap.

Adding magnitudes, 0.30 + 0.12 = 0.42, ignores the sign. Signs matter when you add effects.

Question 14.Interpret · A non-significant interaction

A moderation test gives an interaction coefficient of β = 0.05 (p = .42). What should the researcher conclude?

  • A The moderator must be removed from the model, because it has failed to have any effect
  • B Moderation is confirmed, because the interaction coefficient is positive in sign
  • C No evidence that the effect depends on the moderator, so do not claim moderation ✓ correct
  • D The main effect of the predictor is invalid, because the interaction was not significant

Correct answer C: No evidence that the effect depends on the moderator, so do not claim moderation.

Why this is right.

Moderation is supported only when the interaction term itself is significant. With p = .42 the slope of X → Y does not differ reliably across levels of the moderator. Report that the hypothesis was not supported; the predictor’s main effect is interpreted as usual, and the moderator can stay in the model if theory says it belongs.

The trap.

Do not read a sign without a significance test. A tiny positive coefficient can be pure noise.
