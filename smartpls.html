Section 1
What is SmartPLS?
SmartPLS is the world's leading software for Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM). It is free for academic use, runs on Windows and Mac, and is the tool of choice for management, marketing, hospitality, and social science doctoral research worldwide.
🔑 Why PhD and DBA students use SmartPLS

PLS-SEM works with smaller samples, does not require normally distributed data, and handles both reflective and formative measurement models — making it ideal for management research where perfect conditions rarely exist. It is particularly powerful when your goal is prediction and theory development rather than theory confirmation.

Section 2
PLS-SEM vs CB-SEM — Which One Do You Need?
This is the most common question in doctoral research. The answer depends on your research goals, sample size, data distribution, and measurement model type.
Quick decision: What is your primary research goal?
✦ Choose PLS-SEM (SmartPLS)
  • Prediction and exploration are your goals
  • Sample size is small to medium (n ≥ 30)
  • Data is non-normal or skewed
  • You have formative constructs
  • Your model is complex (many constructs)
  • You are in early theory development
  • Management, hospitality, SME, EdTech research
✦ Choose CB-SEM (AMOS/LISREL)
  • Theory confirmation is your goal
  • Large sample (n ≥ 200 recommended)
  • Data is normally distributed
  • All constructs are reflective only
  • Formative constructs — not suitable
  • Established theory with strong prior evidence
  • Psychology, medicine, economics research

⚠️ Viva Alert — The Question Every Examiner Asks

"Why did you choose PLS-SEM over CB-SEM?" Your answer: "My study is exploratory and prediction-oriented rather than confirmatory. My sample of n=[X] is adequate for PLS-SEM but below the threshold recommended for CB-SEM. Additionally, my model includes both reflective and formative constructs, which CB-SEM cannot accommodate. PLS-SEM is therefore the methodologically appropriate choice as supported by Hair et al. (2019) and Ringle et al. (2023)."

Section 3
Getting Started — Step by Step
Follow these steps exactly. Each links to the official SmartPLS tutorial.
1
Download and Install SmartPLS
Go to smartpls.com/downloads. Choose your OS (Windows or Mac). Install and launch. You get a 30-day free trial of the full version, then the free academic licence covers all essential features.
2
Select a Workspace Folder
SmartPLS stores all your projects in a workspace folder on your computer. Choose a dedicated folder — e.g. My Documents/SmartPLS_Projects. All your models, data files, and results save here automatically. See the official first-steps tutorial.
3
Create Your First Project and Import Data
Click New Project. Name it after your research (e.g. HotelSatisfaction_Study). Import your CSV or Excel data file. SmartPLS reads each column as one indicator variable. Missing values are handled automatically — use Mean Value Replacement for small gaps. See the import data tutorial.
4
Draw Your Path Model
Drag constructs onto the canvas. Double-click a construct to assign its indicators from your dataset. Draw arrows between constructs to represent hypothesised relationships. Arrows from construct to indicators = reflective. Arrows from indicators to construct = formative. See the path model tutorial.
5
Run PLS Algorithm — Assess Measurement Model
Click Calculate → PLS Algorithm. Check outer loadings (≥ 0.70), Composite Reliability (≥ 0.70), AVE (≥ 0.50), and discriminant validity (HTMT < 0.90). Remove items with loadings below 0.40 only if theoretically justified. Never delete items blindly to improve numbers.
6
Run Bootstrapping — Assess Structural Model
Click Calculate → Bootstrapping. Set 5,000 subsamples. Check path coefficients (β), t-values (> 1.96 for p < 0.05), and p-values. Check R² values for each endogenous construct. This is where your hypotheses are supported or rejected. Report confidence intervals from the bootstrapping results.
7
Test Mediation and Moderation (if applicable)
For mediation: check the indirect effect in bootstrapping results. If the confidence interval does not include zero — significant mediation. For moderation: create an interaction term (IV × Moderator) and draw an arrow to the DV. Significant path coefficient = moderation confirmed. See the moderated mediation tutorial.
8
Report Your Results
SmartPLS generates a complete HTML report. Export path diagrams as images. Report in this order: (1) measurement model assessment, (2) discriminant validity, (3) structural model results, (4) hypothesis summary table. Always cite Hair et al. (2019) as your methodological authority.
Section 4
Key Thresholds — What the Numbers Mean
These are the acceptance thresholds used in SmartPLS assessment. Memorise these before your viva.
≥ 0.70
Outer Loadings
Each indicator's loading on its construct. Below 0.40 = consider removing. 0.40–0.70 = keep if AVE is acceptable.
≥ 0.70
Composite Reliability
Internal consistency. 0.70–0.90 = satisfactory. Above 0.95 = possible redundancy.
≥ 0.50
AVE
Average Variance Extracted. Convergent validity. Construct explains more than 50% of indicator variance.
< 0.90
HTMT Ratio
Heterotrait-Monotrait ratio. Discriminant validity. Below 0.85 is conservative threshold.
≥ 0.26
R² Substantial
Variance explained in endogenous construct. 0.26 = substantial, 0.13 = moderate, 0.02 = weak.
5,000
Bootstrap Samples
Standard for bootstrapping in SmartPLS. More samples = more stable results. Never use less than 1,000.
< 5.0
VIF (Formative)
Variance Inflation Factor for formative constructs. Above 5.0 = multicollinearity problem.
> 1.96
t-value (p<0.05)
From bootstrapping. t > 1.96 = significant at 5%. t > 2.576 = significant at 1%.

Section 7 — Class Exercise
Hands-On: The Deli99 Case Study
We use the Deli99 dataset in class. This is a real customer satisfaction survey from a food service company. Download it, import it, and follow along.
📋 The Deli99 Dataset

The dataset contains customer responses on food quality, service quality, atmosphere, value for money, and overall satisfaction. Your task: build a PLS-SEM model predicting customer satisfaction and loyalty. Test which antecedents have the strongest paths. Run bootstrapping and report your hypothesis results.

1
Download the Deli99 CSV
Download from the class materials section on the home page after registration.
2
Import into SmartPLS
File → Import Data File → select Deli99.csv. SmartPLS auto-detects all columns. Verify the data looks correct in the Data View before building your model.
3
Build the Conceptual Model
Create 5 constructs: Food Quality (FQ), Service Quality (SQ), Atmosphere (ATM), Value for Money (VAL), Customer Satisfaction (SAT). Draw paths: FQ → SAT, SQ → SAT, ATM → SAT, VAL → SAT. Assign indicators to each construct from the dataset columns.
4
Run PLS Algorithm and Check Measurement Model
Check all outer loadings ≥ 0.70, CR ≥ 0.70, AVE ≥ 0.50, HTMT < 0.90. Remove any problematic indicators with theoretical justification. Document your decisions.
5
Run Bootstrapping and Report Results
5,000 subsamples. Report path coefficients, t-values, p-values, and R² for Customer Satisfaction. Which antecedent has the strongest effect? Which hypothesis is rejected? Write up your findings in a 3-sentence conclusion.
